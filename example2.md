> What is a rainbow?

A rainbow is a natural phenomenon caused by the refraction, reflection, and dispersion of sunlight within water droplets in the air. When sunlight enters a raindrop, it bends and spreads across the surface, creating a circular pattern of colors at the bottom of the drop. As the sunlight exits the drop, it disperses into the air, creating the colors that are visible to the human eye—typically red, orange, yellow, green, blue, indigo, and violet.

Rainbows usually appear at certain angles relative to the horizon, most commonly at around 42 degrees to the left of the sun and 30 degrees to the right of the horizon. The colors that dominate a rainbow are usually red at the longest wavelengths, but there are variations in the colors based on the intensity of sunlight and the conditions of the viewing angle.

A rainbow is a spectacular natural phenomenon that occurs when sunlight interacts with water droplets in the atmosphere. It typically appears when the sun is positioned behind a group of large, flat, horizontally-angled droplets (often formed by rainstorms) in the sky. This interaction causes the light to bend or reflect, creating a colorful arc across the sky in a particular order depending on the position of the sun relative to the droplets.  

The colors usually form in sequence: red at the top, violet at the bottom, with pink and yellow sometimes accompanying them. Rainbows are commonly seen in warm weather or in regions with abundant rain, and they are often associated with rainbows seen during sunset or early evening.

A rainbow is a natural phenomenon that occurs when sunlight interacts with water droplets in the atmosphere. It appears as a vibrant arc of colors, typically in the sky, caused by the refraction, reflection, and dispersion of light. The colors usually appear in a sequence: red at the top, orange at the middle, yellow at the center, green at the bottom, and finally blue at the bottom.

Rainbows are formed when sunlight enters a small water droplet, which causes the light to bend (refract) as it passes through the droplet. The light then reflects off the inner surface of the droplet and refracts again as it exits, creating the colorful arc. The angle at which the light is refracted and reflected determines the colors visible in a rainbow.
